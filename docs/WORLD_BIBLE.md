# World Bible

Developer-only continuity reference. This file is not copied into the production site.

## Canon status

Facts below are established unless marked **tentative**. When a public record intentionally conflicts with canon, record the conflict and its narrative reason here.

## Agency foundation

- Full name: Federal Unexplained Investigative Division (FUID).
- Nature: a fictional civilian federal investigative division. It is not part of any real agency.
- Public remit: coordination and archival review of incidents that remain unresolved after ordinary jurisdictional processes are exhausted or become contradictory.
- Operating posture: FUID treats anomalies as administrative facts to document, contain, refer, or close—not as proof of a single paranormal worldview.
- Motto: none established. Do not invent one casually.
- Headquarters: **tentative**; not yet named.

## Public organization

- Office of Field Investigations (OFI): receives referrals, conducts interviews and site work, and assembles public case records.
- Other departments are not yet canon. Add them only with content that needs them.

## Classification and status vocabulary

Current public access marking:

- `PUBLIC RELEASE`: approved for the public archive.

Current internal access marking:

- `FUID INTERNAL // CONTROLLED`: story-only administrative control. It is not a claim of real secrecy or secure storage.

Current case statuses:

- `Open — Monitoring`: active observation without a continuous field presence.
- `Closed — Explained`: closed by a documented conventional finding.
- `Closed — Unresolved`: investigation ended without an adequate finding.

Additional terms require a schema and world-bible update before use.

## Record conventions

- Public case numbers follow `FUID-<state>-<year>-<sequence>`, for example `FUID-CO-2004-014`.
- Public document numbers append a type code and sequence, for example `FUID-CO-2004-014-FR-01` for field report 01.
- Internal stable IDs are lowercase namespaced identifiers and never change after release.

- 1972-01-14: FUID closes the Bellglass investigation unresolved after orchard clearance reveals no den, passage, or structural route and six months pass without a confirmed recurrence.
- 1986-08-27: End-of-season inventory at Harrow Ridge Fire Station identifies a fourth work position and shift entries for R. Vale despite authorization for three observers.

- 2004-11-18: A maintenance crew reports repeated road-distance discrepancies on a service road near Bellwether Reservoir, Colorado.

## Intentional uncertainties

- The measured extra distance in the Bellwether case has no established cause.
- The missing tire impressions, duplicate-marker statement, fixed-camera omission, and unauthored mileage corrections are observations without an established common cause.
- The record does not establish that the anomaly is supernatural, connected to another case, or part of a broader program.
- Harrow Station's fourth desk, shared asset number, inconsistent recollections of R. Vale, and maintained path are established observations without an identified common cause.
- The three Harrow witnesses' controlled statements are canon. Their memories conflict internally but were recorded independently; no witness can describe Vale or place all four observers together.
- The unsigned 1994 Harrow claimant letter is inconclusive and contains no verified private knowledge. It does not establish that R. Vale returned or wrote it.
- The March 19 property-storage incident is canon and represents a direct physical anomaly, not merely a record contradiction. The caller's identity, origin, knowledge of Keene, and meaning of “relief” remain unresolved.
- No canonical connection exists between Harrow Station and Bellwether Reservoir.
- The Ash Creek siren is fully explained and is not a hidden paranormal event. It demonstrates that FUID sometimes reaches an ordinary finding.
- The Saint Orison drainage animal is a real, physically observed but taxonomically unidentified animal. Its flag-sorting behavior and culvert route are unresolved. “Ledgerback” is unofficial and does not establish a species.
- The pump-cabinet signal and rearranged tags are established observations. The record does not establish whether the animal produced the remote signal or entered the cabinet.
- The Lantern Ridge track and temperature observations are canon, but the animal's reported height and means of culvert passage are unverified. It has no canonical connection to the Saint Orison animal or any other case.
- The controlled timing discrepancy and unsupported antler tips are canon observations, not proof that the animal occupied two locations or traveled backward in time.
- The Bellglass Visitor was physically observed but never fully photographed or taxonomically identified. Its apparent narrowing against a tree, discontinuous tracks, and the interior-first alteration of fruit are canon; the record does not establish that it moved through trees or solid material.
- The recorded instruction “leave the last one” and the warm final fruit are canon. The voice is not identified as the animal, a person, or a shared witness effect.
- The Morrow Cut voices, anticipatory gate sequence, sealed-capsule condensation, and recovered primary teeth are canon. The teeth do not match site personnel or the missing-person references available at the time, but they are not canonically linked to a crime, death, mine disaster, or another FUID case.
- Bellglass and Morrow Cut have no canonical connection to each other or to the earlier archive. Repeated boundary and sequence motifs are thematic contrast, not evidence of one cause.

## Tone and anomaly range

The archive should preserve contrast. Ash Creek demonstrates an ordinary explanation; Bellwether emphasizes measurement and observation; Saint Orison and Lantern Ridge provide unusual-animal fieldwork; Bellglass adds an uncanny cryptid with concrete biological evidence; Harrow and Morrow Cut include direct, frightening contact events of different kinds. Future cases should continue mixing low-risk oddity, procedural unease, physically impossible evidence, and occasional credible fear. Do not reduce every anomaly to contradictory paperwork, and do not make every severe event part of one conspiracy.
